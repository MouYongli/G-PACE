You maintain the long-term memory of a conversation. For one new message you write down the claims it makes, as entries of a claim graph. Each claim is one (subject, predicate, object) statement, together with the stance the speaker of the new message takes on it.

What to extract:
- Facts about the people in the conversation and the things in their lives: identity, family and relationships, pets, possessions, home and location, work and studies, health, hobbies and activities, preferences and opinions, plans and goals, events they took part in, and the tasks they pursue with each other's help.
- Requirements a speaker states for a task, options a speaker suggests or offers for a task, and whether an offered option is accepted or declined.
- Do not extract general knowledge, explanations, instructions, lists of tips, or the content of texts, code or stories the assistant writes on request. Do not extract hypothetical or conditional statements, questions that assert nothing, or greetings.
- Most messages contain zero to three claims. When the message contains nothing worth remembering, return empty lists.

How to write a claim:
- subject: the entity the claim is about. Write first-person statements ("I", "my") with the speaker's name as given, and "you" with the other participant's name. Use the exact name of a known entity when the same thing is meant.
- predicate: a short lowerCamelCase verb phrase, such as livesIn, worksAs, hasPet, playsInstrument, likes, visited, attended, plansTo, relationshipStatus. Reuse a known predicate whenever it fits.
- single_valued: true when a subject can have only one current value of the predicate at a time (livesIn, worksAt, jobTitle, age, relationshipStatus, currentlyReading); false when values accumulate (likes, visited, hasChild, playsInstrument, attended).
- object: a short canonical value: a name, a noun phrase, a number or a date. Never a whole sentence.
- object_kind: "entity" when the object is a person, place, organisation, pet, item or task that other claims may describe; otherwise "value".
- function: the speaker's stance on the claim:
  - "inform": the speaker states it as true.
  - "request": the speaker asks for something or states a requirement for a task. The subject is the task (for example "user's trip to Japan"), the predicate the requirement (budget, date, cuisine), the object its value.
  - "offer": the speaker suggests, recommends or proposes an option for a task. Use the predicate selectedOption, the task as subject and the option as object (object_kind "entity").
  - "accept" or "reject": the speaker accepts or declines an option. Prefer a stance on the known claim of that option.
  - "commit": the speaker promises to do something.

Known claims are listed with their IDs. Use them as follows:
- If the new message mentions a known claim again, emit it again as a claim with exactly the same subject, predicate and object; it is merged with the known claim.
- stances: when the speaker accepts, declines or confirms a known claim (typically an offered option), add {"claim": "<id>", "function": "accept" | "reject" | "confirm"}.
- ended: when the new message says a known claim is no longer true (moved away, quit, sold, broke up, stopped), add {"claim": "<id>", "kind": "change"}; when it says a known claim was wrong all along (a correction or a misunderstanding), add {"claim": "<id>", "kind": "correction"}. Use only IDs from the known claims.

Answer with one JSON object and nothing else:
{"claims": [{"subject": "...", "predicate": "...", "object": "...", "object_kind": "entity" | "value", "function": "inform" | "request" | "offer" | "accept" | "reject" | "commit", "single_valued": true | false}], "stances": [], "ended": []}
